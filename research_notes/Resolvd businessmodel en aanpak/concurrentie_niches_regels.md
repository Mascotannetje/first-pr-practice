# Competitive landscape, underserved niches, regulation and channels for AI implementation and automation for Dutch SMEs (mkb), 2024–2026

> Method note for the report writer: research done on 22 Oct 2026 with web search. Direct page fetches of Dutch domains (cbs.nl, autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl, anko.nl, frankwatching.com) and the CBS OData API failed from this environment (DNS/proxy errors). So most facts below come from search-result extracts of the cited pages, not from full-page reads. Prices are vendor marketing figures and may exclude VAT. Spot-check the most important numbers on the URLs before publishing.

## 1. Which Dutch AI and automation agencies and freelancers target the mkb, what do they offer, and at what prices?

### Takeaway
The NL market for "AI for the mkb" is already crowded with small agencies and productised AI-receptionist (voice agent) start-ups. Public prices cluster around €1,500–8,000 one-off for custom workflows or chatbots, and €50–700 per month for AI phone answering. Generic "AI agency for the mkb" positioning is therefore not defensible. Vertical packaging (such as Van Borg for construction and installation, or VakmanAI for tradespeople) is already starting to appear.

### Cited Findings
**AI receptionist / AI telephony providers active in NL (public prices):**
- Voicelabs: €149/month excl. VAT with 300 AI call minutes included; "Groei" plan €299/month with 1,000 minutes. Its mkb page states an entry price "from €99/month" — [Voicelabs prijzen](https://www.voicelabs.nl/prijzen); [Voicelabs voor mkb](https://www.voicelabs.nl/voor-mkb)
- AI Receptionisten (phone and WhatsApp AI): €50/month excl. VAT with 200 appointments included, cancellable monthly. It claims a human telephonist costs "€2,500+ per month" — [ai-receptionisten.nl](https://ai-receptionisten.nl/); [Telefoniste inhuren kosten 2026](https://ai-receptionisten.nl/blog/telefoniste-inhuren-kosten)
- Aanloop AI ("Emma"; brands itself "AI Bureau Nederland voor het MKB"): from €149/month with a money-back guarantee — [aanloopai.nl](https://aanloopai.nl/)
- Van Borg: AI receptionist aimed specifically at construction and installation firms, fixed monthly fee from €495, cancellable monthly after 3 months — [Van Borg bouw-installatie](https://vanborg.com/sectoren/bouw-installatie)
- Utomatic: €3,950 one-off plus €349/month (per its own page) — [Utomatic AI receptionist Nederland](https://utomatic.ai/en/posts/ai-receptionist-nederland)

- VakmanAI (sells to tradespeople and publishes a price comparison): simple answering services €15–35/month; AI telephonist with dashboard and integrations €99–299/month; all-in systems for trade businesses with quote follow-up and WhatsApp €197–697/month — [VakmanAI: Wat kost een AI-receptionist?](https://vakmanai.nl/wat-kost-een-ai-receptionist/)
- Human answering service benchmark: BizziPhone €75–300/month (as cited by a competitor) — [VakmanAI](https://vakmanai.nl/wat-kost-een-ai-receptionist/)

**Treatwell (salon platform) entered AI receptionist itself:**
- Treatwell launched an "AI Receptionist" for salons across European markets (Sept 2026). It handles calls, bookings, cancellations and client queries, with subscriptions from £69/month in three tiers. WhatsApp answering is listed as "coming soon". It is not confirmed whether it is live in NL — [IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/treatwell-launches-ai-receptionist-for-salons-across-europe); [Treatwell partners: AI Receptionist](https://www.treatwell.co.uk/partners?p=30080)

The search summary does not say which figure belongs to which agency; check each page.
- Other examples of agencies explicitly targeting the mkb with AI and automation: [Breinpro](https://www.breinpro.ai/), [Codelevate](https://www.codelevate.com/nl/blog/hoe-kies-je-een-ai-partner-voor-je-mkb), [Levtics](https://levtics.com/blog/beste-ai-automation-tools-mkb-nederland), [Timmermans Media](https://www.timmermansmedia.nl/blog/ai/n8n-vs-make-vs-zapier-vergelijking/), [aiagentslatenmaken.nl](https://aiagentslatenmaken.nl/), [Cruxdigits](https://cruxdigits.nl/ai-automation/)

### Inferences
- The AI receptionist segment is price-competitive: entry is €50–150/month, so a student startup cannot win on generic voice agents. Margin comes from vertical integration, such as booking into the sector tool, quote follow-up and WhatsApp, plus setup and maintenance.
- Vertical players are emerging (Van Borg and VakmanAI for construction and trades; Treatwell for salons). The niche window for "AI receptionist for X" is closing in sectors where the dominant SaaS vendor builds it in, such as salons via Treatwell. It is still open where the sector tool is fragmented, such as installers, garages and physiotherapy.
- Agency project prices (€1,500–8,000) suggest Resolvd could compete with a fixed-price productised offer around €1,000–2,500 setup plus a retainer, if it is narrowly scoped. This is an inference, not a sourced benchmark.

### Gaps
- No official Zapier or Make partner directory listing for NL, and no partner tier economics, was retrieved.
- No reliable count of the number of Dutch AI agencies or freelancers was found.
- Freelancer day rates for AI/automation in NL were not found in this research pass.

## 2. Which sector SaaS tools already cover parts of this, and where do they leave gaps?

### Takeaway
Sector SaaS covers booking, inbox and administration well. AI features are mostly bolt-ons: per-conversation AI surcharges (Trengo), a small AI-agent package (Watermelon), MCP/API access (Moneybird), or rule-based automation without LLMs (Salonized). The gap is cross-tool orchestration, for example phone or WhatsApp to booking or quote to invoice to follow-up across 2–3 systems, plus sector-specific Dutch-language agents. That is where custom agents and integrations can add value.

### Cited Findings
- **Trengo** (Dutch omnichannel inbox): Boost €299/month annually (€349 monthly), Pro €499/month annually (€599 monthly). The AI assistant is included but each AI-handled conversation costs extra, about €0.25–0.30. Sources differ on the exact surcharge — [Stewie: Trengo vs Zendesk](https://stewie.io/blog/trengo-vs-zendesk); [eesel.ai: Trengo pricing](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/trengo-pricing); [Chatimize review](https://chatimize.com/reviews/trengo/)
- **Watermelon** (Dutch AI chatbot platform; launched a ChatGPT-based platform in March 2023): Starter €99/month (or €1,008/year) with 250 conversations/month and at least one AI agent, aimed at small teams. Premium is reportedly €215/month annually (third-party source) — [Watermelon docs: Starter](https://watermelon.ai/docs/help-center/plans-pricing/watermelon-starter.md); [Fitgap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/watermelon); [Dutch IT Channel 2023](https://www.dutchitchannel.nl/news/221968/ai-startup-watermelon-lanceert-chatbotplatform-met-chatgpt)
- **Salonized** (run by Treatwell Salonized NL B.V., part of the Treatwell group): a June 2026 review says it has no LLM/ML AI in the narrow sense. Its strength is rule-based automation (online booking, automatic reminders). Prices: appointments €21/month, Basic €39/month, Pro €59/month — [KI-Syndikat Salonized review](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/salonized/)
- **Treatwell** now sells an AI phone receptionist that books into the Treatwell calendar (see Q1) — [Treatwell partners](https://www.treatwell.co.uk/partners?p=30080)
- **Moneybird** launched an MCP-based "AI-koppeling" (2025) that lets ChatGPT, Claude, Mistral and others read or write the administration. Read-only versus read-write is set by the MCP address — [Moneybird helpcenter: AI-koppeling](https://helpcenter.moneybird.nl/nl/articles/396511-ai-koppeling); [Softwarepakketten.nl](https://www.softwarepakketten.nl/bericht/8436&bronw=1/Moneybird_lanceert_AI_koppeling_via_MCP.htm). Moneybird markets "agentic boekhouden", including creating an invoice when a quote is accepted — [Moneybird agentic boekhouden](https://www.moneybird.be/product/agentic-boekhouden/); [Moneybird blog: API's, webhooks en AI](https://www.moneybird.nl/blog/apis-webhooks-en-ai-automatiseer-je-boekhouding/)
- **Gripp** (project, quote, planning, hours and invoicing software for agencies and technical firms) is a target of third-party "AI coupling" builders, which indicates that native AI is limited — [Rawrr Media](https://rawrrmedia.com/ai-koppelingen/); [APIboard](https://www.apiboard.nl/koppelingen/moneybird) (lists Gripp, AFAS, Exact Online, Twinfield, Moneybird, SnelStart, e-Boekhouden, Teamleader, Shopify and others)

### Inferences
- **Gap A, phone and WhatsApp to the sector system:** inbox tools (Trengo, Watermelon) handle chat or email, but booking or quoting lives in sector tools. In fragmented sectors (installers, garages, physiotherapy) nobody owns the "call, then a structured job or appointment in the planning tool" link. That is the clearest gap.
- **Gap B, quote follow-up and admin glue:** Moneybird's MCP and API make it cheap to build agents on top of the administration. Few SMEs can set this up themselves, so it suits a service model of configuration plus support rather than new SaaS.
- **Risk:** where one vendor dominates a vertical (Treatwell/Salonized in beauty), that vendor can launch the same feature natively, as Treatwell already has. A small agency should avoid head-on competition there.

### Gaps
- No primary data retrieved on Exact Online's or e-Boekhouden's AI features.
- No primary data on installer field-service and quoting tools (for example Tradecount or other werkbon/quote software) or their AI features. The search returned nothing usable.
- Pricing pages for Trengo, Salonized and Gripp were not fetched directly (fetch failures). Figures come from third-party reviews.

## 3. Which Dutch sectors with many SMEs have high repetitive customer contact and low digital maturity?

### Takeaway
AI adoption among Dutch micro firms is still low: 13.8% of firms with 2–9 employees used AI in 2025, against 29.8% for firms with 10–249 employees and 66.2% for large firms. Sectors with large SME counts and heavy phone or appointment traffic include hairdressers (about 32,000 firms), building installation (about 42,000 firms, fast-growing), physiotherapy (about 10,000 firms) and garages (about 9,400). These are plausible targets. Installers and garages combine high call volume with a fragmented tool landscape.

### Cited Findings
- **AI adoption (CBS, provisional 2025):** micro firms (2–9 employees) 13.8%; mkb (10–249) 29.8%; large (250+) 66.2%. All firms with 2+ employees: 17% in 2025, against 13% in 2024 and 8% in 2023. CBS counts a firm as a user if it uses at least one of eight AI technologies — [CBS longread: gebruik van AI-technologie door Nederlandse microbedrijven (2026)](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/longread/rapportages/2026/gebruik-van-ai-technologie-door-nederlandse-microbedrijven/2-gebruik-ai-technologie); [ICT Magazine: "Slechts 14 procent van Nederlandse microbedrijven gebruikt AI"](https://www.ictmagazine.nl/nieuws/slechts-14-procent-van-nederlandse-microbedrijven-gebruikt-ai/); [MKB Servicedesk](https://www.mkbservicedesk.nl/nieuws/ondernemersnieuws/hoe-gebruiken-nederlandse-microbedrijven-ai-technologie-een-overzicht-van). Figures are from search extracts; the CBS page itself could not be fetched.
- A government-commissioned study, "Onderzoek AI-gebruik in het mkb: Ambitie of aarzeling?", is published on open.overheid.nl — [open.overheid.nl](https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/30ab7be6-a0ea-4e4f-bf86-91157c56ccc8/file) (contents not reviewed)
- **Hairdressers (SBI 96021):** 31,920 firms (Monitor Kerndata Kappers 2025, based on CBS). The number of hairdressing firms grew 15.8% over five years — [Brancheplatform Kappers: Monitor Kerndata Kappers 2025 (PDF)](https://brancheplatformkappers.nl/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Rapportage-Monitor-Kerndata-Kappers-editie-2025.pdf); [Marktdata](https://marktdata.nl/aanhoudende-groei-aantal-kapperszaken/). The broader hair and beauty group (SBI 96.02) counts 79,205 firms in Q1 2026 — [Firmfocus](https://www.firmfocus.biz/NL/BI/branche/kappers-schoonheidsalons)
- **Building installation:** firm count grew from 28,425 (2021) to 42,410 (2026) per CBS, with electrical installers growing fastest (secondary source) — [Searchlab: bouw en installatie statistieken 2026](https://searchlab.nl/statistieken/bouw-installatie-marketing-statistieken-2026)
- **Physiotherapy:** firms with physiotherapy as main activity grew from 9,485 (2019) to 10,350 (2024). The number of locations rose 15.9%, with chain formation — [Marktdata: fysiotherapie](https://marktdata.nl/groei-aantal-praktijken-en-ketenvorming-in-de-fysiotherapie/)
- **Garages:** about 9,400–9,500 garages in the KVK register (commercial data provider, not official) — [companydata.com](https://companydata.com/netherlands/garages/). BOVAG says it represents "ruim 8.000" businesses in mobility — [BOVAG: over BOVAG](https://www.bovag.nl/over-bovag). Another source says about 11,000 members; the counts conflict — [Garage Dijkzicht](https://www.dijkzicht.nl/6/Bovag/)
- Official CBS tables for counts by SBI 2025 sector: [CBS 86280NED Bedrijven; bedrijfstak (SBI 2025)](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/cijfers/detail/86280NED); by size: [CBS 86281NED](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/cijfers/detail/86281NED); [CBS maatwerk: bedrijven en werknemers naar bedrijfsgrootte 2024](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/maatwerk/2026/15/aantallen-bedrijven-en-werknemers-naar-bedrijfsgrootte-2024)
- Evidence of demand and competition in installers: dedicated AI receptionists for construction and installation (Van Borg, VakmanAI), and Techniek Nederland listing an event on AI agent developments for the installation sector — [Van Borg](https://vanborg.com/sectoren/bouw-installatie); [VakmanAI](https://vakmanai.nl/wat-kost-een-ai-receptionist/); [Techniek Nederland home](https://www.technieknederland.nl/)

### Inferences
- **Most promising niche candidates for Resolvd** (inference from counts, the tool landscape and competition):
  1. **Installers (plumbing, HVAC, heat pumps, electrical)**: about 42,000 firms, high inbound call volume (breakdowns, quotes), fragmented quoting and planning tools. There is competition from Van Borg and VakmanAI, but it is early-stage. Differentiate on integration with quoting, werkbon and invoicing (for example Moneybird or e-Boekhouden) and quote follow-up.
  2. **Garages (independent, non-dealer)**: about 9,400 firms, repetitive calls (APK, service appointments, "is my car ready?"). No garage-specific AI receptionist was found in this research, which makes it a possible underserved niche (to verify).
  3. **Physiotherapy**: about 10,350 firms, appointment-heavy, but health data is a special category under the AVG (higher compliance burden; see Q4). Chains are consolidating, so buyers are getting larger.
  4. **Salons**: large count (about 32,000 hairdressers), but Treatwell/Salonized dominate and now ship AI reception. Lower defensibility.
- Micro firms (13.8% AI use) are the least served but also have the smallest budgets. Firms with 5–50 staff are probably the sweet spot.

### Gaps
- No official counts were retrieved for real-estate agents (makelaars), webshops or hospitality (horeca); look these up in CBS 86280NED by SBI code.
- No sector-level data on call or contact volumes, or on digital maturity per sector (for example a CBS ICT-use breakdown by sector), was retrieved.
- The CBS micro-firm AI longread includes sector breakdowns, but they could not be read because the fetch failed.

## 4. What regulations matter (AVG/GDPR, EU AI Act, consumer rules) and what do they mean in practice for a small agency?

### Takeaway
Three layers apply. (1) AVG: the client SME is controller and Resolvd (and the LLM vendor) are processors, so a verwerkersovereenkomst is needed down the chain. US LLM transfers currently rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, which was upheld by the General Court in Sept 2025 but is under appeal at the CJEU. (2) AI Act Article 50: from 2 August 2026, people must be told they are interacting with an AI system. The Digital Omnibus did not postpone this for chatbots. (3) Consumer law: per AP and ACM, companies must stay reachable by a human. AP/ACM guidance on chatbots was expected in autumn 2026.

### Cited Findings
**AVG / GDPR:**
- Dutch guidance sources state that a business using an AI supplier with customer personal data needs a processing agreement (verwerkersovereenkomst). Some claim the AP treats entering personal data into chatbots without one as a data breach; this AP position was not confirmed in a primary AP source — [Teamleader](https://www.teamleader.eu/nl/blog/ai-gebruiken-in-je-mkb-zo-bescherm-je-privacy-en-klantdata-in-7-stappen); [Digitaalnu: AI-chatbot en de AVG](https://digitaalnu.nl/blog/ai-chatbot-en-de-avg); [Invenix](https://invenix.nl/chatgpt-veiligheid-mkb/)
- OpenAI offers an Article 28 GDPR DPA for Business, Enterprise and API, not for personal (Free/Plus) accounts, which by default may use inputs for training — [Invenix](https://invenix.nl/chatgpt-veiligheid-mkb/); [Yspeert: rol OpenAI en gebruiker](https://yspeert.nl/yspeert-learning/kennisbank/chatgpt-en-de-avg-wie-is-verantwoordelijk-voor-het-voldoen-aan-de-verplichtingen-uit-de-avg/)
- **US transfers:** the EU General Court dismissed the Latombe challenge on 3 Sept 2025 and upheld the EU-US Data Privacy Framework adequacy decision (T-553/23) — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/european-general-court-dismisses-latombe-challenge-upholds-eu-us-data-privacy-framework); Latombe appealed to the CJEU on 31 Oct 2025 (C-703/25 P), so the DPF faces renewed legal risk — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20251201-european-court-of-justice-to-review-challenge-to-eu-us-data-privacy-framework); [Privacy Company](https://www.privacycompany.eu/blog/latombe-v-european-commission-the-dpf-ca-tombe); [heise](https://heise.de/-10965203)
- Practical advice in Dutch sources: ask where data is processed, which transfer basis applies outside the EU (DPF or SCCs), and whether training on customer data is disabled. Prefer EU data residency where possible — [Conversal](https://www.conversal.be/blog/gdpr-en-ai-tools-welke-data-mag-je-voeren-aan-chatgpt/); [Monochat](https://monochat.ai/nl/blog/ai-chatbot-data-privacy)

**EU AI Act:**
- Article 50(1) (inform people they are interacting with an AI system) applies from 2 August 2026 and was **not** postponed by the Digital Omnibus — [Frankwatching, 8 Sept 2026](https://www.frankwatching.com/archive/2026/09/08/ai-transparantie-verplicht-artikel-50/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Pilex](https://www.pilex.ai/kennisbank/eu-ai-act-uitstel-wat-geldt-nu)
- The Digital Omnibus (reported as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, published 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026) moves Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 Dec 2027, and high-risk AI in regulated products to Aug 2028. Generative AI providers already on the market before 2 Aug 2026 get until 2 Dec 2026 for machine-readable marking (Art. 50(2)) — [ActCheck](https://actcheck.nl/blog-ai-act-digital-omnibus-uitstel); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Praxikon](https://www.praxikon.com/en/posts/digital-omnibus-ai-act-what-changes-what-now-2026). The regulation number was not verified on EUR-Lex.
- In practice the AI disclosure must be visible at the start of the contact — [DMCC](https://dmcc.nl/ai-chatbots-en-klantcontact-handig-maar-let-op-de-transparantie-verplichtingen/); per-paragraph overview — [AI Compliance Hub](https://www.aicompliancehub.nl/ai-act-transparantieplicht); [aiactblog.nl](https://www.aiactblog.nl/en/posts/article-50-transparency-obligations-practical-2026)
- Older sources (2025) suggested parts of Article 50 might shift. Later sources (Aug–Sept 2026) state firmly that they did not, and the later sources are preferred — [Pilex](https://www.pilex.ai/kennisbank/eu-ai-act-uitstel-wat-geldt-nu)

**Consumer rules / automated customer service:**
- ACM and AP jointly called on companies (Oct 2025, "Laat de mens bereikbaar blijven") not to let AI chatbots fully replace customer service. They say consumer law already requires that consumers can contact the company directly and effectively, so a human must remain reachable — [AP/ACM oproep AI-chatbots (PDF)](https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/system/files?file=2025-10%2FOproep+ACM+en+AP+AI-chatbots.pdf); [Personeelsnet](https://www.personeelsnet.nl/bericht/chatbot-inzetten-contact-met-goed-opgeleide-medewerker-blijft-verplicht); [Privacyteam](https://www.privacyteam.nl/nieuws/chatbot-mag-klantenservice-niet-volledig-overnemen)
- AP and ACM are preparing guidance on responsible chatbot use, expected in autumn 2026. A Consumentenbond study called chatbots an "obstacle" — [ICT Magazine](https://www.ictmagazine.nl/nieuws/onderzoek-consumentenbond-chatbot-is-obstakel-geworden/)
- The EU Digital Fairness Act is still in preparation and may add rules for chat-based customer service — [Personeelsnet](https://www.personeelsnet.nl/bericht/chatbot-inzetten-contact-met-goed-opgeleide-medewerker-blijft-verplicht)
- No Dutch bill creating a statutory "right to a human" in customer service was found. California AB 1609 is a foreign example — [Klantcontact.nl](https://www.klantcontact.nl/in-de-media/wordt-menselijke-klantenservice-een-wettelijk-recht/)

### Inferences (practical checklist for a small agency)
- Sign a verwerkersovereenkomst with every client (Resolvd as processor). List sub-processors (LLM vendor, telephony, hosting) and use only business/API tiers with a DPA and training switched off.
- Prefer EU-hosted model endpoints or EU data residency where available, because the DPF is under CJEU appeal. Document the transfer basis (DPF plus SCCs as fallback).
- Build AI disclosure into every voice or chat agent's opening line ("You are speaking with a digital assistant of …"), plus an always-available human escalation (call-back, transfer or WhatsApp handover). This is compliance by default and can be a selling point.
- Avoid or price in extra compliance for health data (physiotherapy) and other special categories. Run a DPIA where needed.
- Customer-service agents for SMEs are generally not "high-risk" under Annex III, so the main AI Act duties for Resolvd are Article 50 transparency and AI literacy. The AI-literacy duty (Article 4, applicable since Feb 2025) is from training knowledge and was not sourced in this pass.

### Gaps
- The AP's own guidance pages on AI chatbots and verwerkersovereenkomsten could not be fetched. The claim that "the AP treats this as a data breach" is unverified.
- The status of the AP/ACM chatbot guidance (expected autumn 2026) as of 22 Oct 2026 is unknown.
- The CJEU timetable for C-703/25 P is unknown.
- No source retrieved on rules for recording or transcribing phone calls (consent and information duties) for AI voice agents in NL; this deserves follow-up.

## 5. Which partnership or sales channels work in NL?

### Takeaway
The most concrete channels found are: (1) regional "Digitale Werkplaatsen" and AI labs that connect students with mkb firms (free AI scans, projects), which fits a student startup well; (2) branch associations with member-benefit or partner programmes (Techniek Nederland, ANKO, BOVAG, KHN); (3) accountants, who are under pressure to digitise and want an advisory role but lack AI capacity in practice; (4) sector SaaS ecosystems (Moneybird API/MCP, integration marketplaces). Evidence that any of these channels converts for AI agencies specifically is thin.

### Cited Findings
**Student and innovation programmes:**
- MKB Digiwerkplaats (Haaglanden): students from De Haagse Hogeschool, MBO Rijnland and ROC Mondriaan help mkb firms free of charge with digitisation, including AI. It offers a free AI pre-scan and runs a project "Had ik maar een dashboard" (Nov 2026–Jan 2027) — [mkbdigiwerkplaats.nl](https://mkbdigiwerkplaats.nl/); [Studenten ontdekken hoe AI ondernemers verder helpt](https://mkbdigiwerkplaats.nl/knowledgebase/studenten-ontdekken-hoe-ai-ondernemers-verder-helpt)

### Inferences
- **Best fit for a student startup:** join or partner with a Digitale Werkplaats or AI Werklab (Haaglanden is active right now) to get warm mkb leads via free AI scans, then convert to paid implementations. Check the programmes' rules on commercial follow-up first.
- **Vertical channel:** if Resolvd picks installers, the Techniek Nederland member-benefit programme is the natural channel (it already lists supplier partners and is engaging with AI agents). For garages, the equivalent is BOVAG. For salons, ANKO exists, but the Treatwell overlap makes it less attractive.
- **Accountants as referral partners:** their AI ambition is high but execution lags, so a "we implement, you advise" referral deal (revenue share) is plausible, particularly with Moneybird or e-Boekhouden-oriented small firms. No source confirms this model working in NL.
